# Research Basis — Bell Performance 13.22.0

Bell Performance is a coaching application, not a diagnostic or medical-treatment system. The implementation below uses broad exercise categories from clinical practice guidelines and systematic reviews. It does not claim that one exercise list is universally correct, and clinician restrictions take priority.

## Healthy movement preparation

1. Lauersen JB, Bertelsen DM, Andersen LB. The effectiveness of exercise interventions to prevent sports injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. *Br J Sports Med.* 2014;48(11):871-877. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2013-092538.
   - Bell implication: do not present stretching alone as an injury-prevention program; include strength, balance/proprioception, and multicomponent preparation.

2. McCrary JM, Ackermann BJ, Halaki M. A systematic review of the effects of upper body warm-up on performance and injury. *Br J Sports Med.* 2015;49(14):935-942. PMID:25694615.
   - Bell implication: use dynamic and task-specific upper-body preparation for performance; avoid unsupported promises that a brief warm-up prevents shoulder injury.

3. Alizadeh S, et al. Resistance Training Induces Improvements in Range of Motion: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Sports Med.* 2023. PMID:36622555.
   - Bell implication: loaded controlled movement can contribute to range-of-motion development, so mobility is not limited to passive stretching.

## Condition-specific Rehab Support

1. Martin RL, et al. Ankle Stability and Movement Coordination Impairments: Lateral Ankle Ligament Sprains Revision 2021. *J Orthop Sports Phys Ther.* 2021;51(4):CPG1-CPG80. doi:10.2519/jospt.2021.0302.
   - Bell categories: ankle motion, progressive loading, balance/proprioception, functional progression, and graded return to impact when cleared.

2. Willy RW, et al. Patellofemoral Pain: Clinical Practice Guidelines. *J Orthop Sports Phys Ther.* 2019;49(9):CPG1-CPG95. doi:10.2519/jospt.2019.0302.
   - Bell categories: combined hip- and knee-targeted exercise, with posterolateral hip emphasis reasonable early and progressive knee loading.

3. Desmeules F, et al. Rotator Cuff Tendinopathy Diagnosis, Nonsurgical Medical Care, and Rehabilitation: A Clinical Practice Guideline. *J Orthop Sports Phys Ther.* 2025;55(4):235-274. doi:10.2519/jospt.2025.13182.
   - Bell categories: symptom-informed active exercise, progressive rotator-cuff and scapular loading, load management, and graded return to function/sport.

4. George SZ, et al. Interventions for the Management of Acute and Chronic Low Back Pain: Revision 2021. *J Orthop Sports Phys Ther.* 2021;51(11):CPG1-CPG60. doi:10.2519/jospt.2021.0304.
   - Bell categories: general and aerobic exercise, trunk strengthening/endurance, specific trunk activation, movement control, and trunk mobility based on presentation and tolerance.

## Safety design

- Bell does not infer a formal diagnosis from a pain note.
- Condition-specific modules require the athlete to identify a clinician-diagnosed supported condition.
- Undiagnosed symptoms receive conservative movement support only.
- Saved red-flag symptoms pause app-generated exercise recommendations.
- The app does not provide clearance to return to impact, overhead loading, or sport.
